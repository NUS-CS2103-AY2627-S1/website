Admittedly, if you are someone who normally prepares well for the tutorials and actively takes part in tutorial discussions, our tutorials can feel slow, less efficient, or less active. We hope you still bear with us because the tutorial structure (like many other things in this course) aims to raise the lower bar of the whole class, which is important when there is a major team project (to reduce the skill gap between team members).

The most common concern about this course is the workload. It is also interesting to see peaks around 4, 5, 6, 7, and 8, indicating a big variation in how students perceive the workload of this course.

Why do some students find the workload MUCH higher than others do? Possible reasons:

  1. Possible reason: You are doing more work than others.
    Response: If this is because you are doing the course more sincerely (e.g., not skipping even optional topics/tasks), it will pay off (i.e., you'll learn more), but you might still want to recalibrate the time you spend on this course if you don't have enough time for other courses.
    If this is because you are unaware of ways to reduce the workload (e.g., omitting optional topics), refer to the recommendations given further below.
  2. Possible reason: You need more time than others to complete course tasks because you are an inexperienced coder.
    Response: The good news is, you are getting more value from this course than others (your skills will improve a lot more from the course than others' skills will). But there is an unavoidable price to pay (i.e., the extra time you spend on the course). If this is affecting your other courses, you might want to cut down the work you do in the course, with the understanding that your skills will improve less too.
  3. Possible reason: You are taking shortcuts (e.g., not doing weekly topics before you attempt weekly project tasks to save time, but ending up spending even more time as you lack the knowledge needed to do the task).
    Response: This is concerning and ill-advised (it results in a lose-lose outcome -- less learning, more time wasted). Instead, to reduce the time spent on this course without losing too much value, follow our instructions/schedule closely, but omit optional topics/tasks and stop when you have reached the minimum requirement.

We know the workload concern is real, and we firmly believe that no course should take more than its fair share of students' time. This course aims to teach you SE skills that take a lot of practice, which makes the workload feel heavy, especially if you are a beginner coder.
On our side, we have been cutting down the workload of this course over time. This semester, we have removed some major tP deliverables, and also provided you with a way to earn 2 units through CP3108A/B.

Some recommendations for you:

  • If you struggled with the iP workload, don't be too ambitious with the tP.
    Reminder: there are no marks allocated for making the product interesting, unique, cool, etc.
    Also, keep in mind that quality counts more than quantity. In most cases, quantity is graded on an S/U basis. Aim to do less but do it really well.
    Plan to add/update tP features in small steps (each step leading to a usable and slightly better version of the feature), so that you can stop any time the workload exceeds what you are prepared for.
  • If you don't have time to study all topics, use the topic levels system to figure out which topics are higher priority.

My comments:

Good to see most of you are satisfied with the intensity of the AI+SE aspect of the course.

  • We can't go 'slow' on AI+SE. In an ideal world, we would have focused on learning foundational SE skills first, and then moved on to AI+SE in a later course. But given the current rapid infiltration of AI into SE, we can't afford to go slow on AI+SE. Hence, we try to introduce you to AI+SE in this course itself.
  • The level of AI use can affect time spent and knowledge gained. For example, less AI use -> more time taken to complete a task -> more learning of foundational skills, less learning of AI+SE skills. However, our grading scheme is designed to ensure your marks are not affected by the use of more expensive AI tools or heavier use of AI. After all, students did these same learning tasks with zero AI use a few semesters ago.
  • We trust you to decide how deep to go into AI+SE. How much AI to use, for which tasks, and in what way depends on your own skills, learning goals, and career goals.
